### Added — library scan, TMDB matching and subtitles

CineFilm can now read the real films. A scan walks the library folder (`CINEFILM_LIBRARY_ROOT`, stored paths are relative to it and anything that escapes it is refused) for `.m4v`, `.mp4` and `.mov` files, with `.avi` and `.mkv` recorded as needing conversion, reads each with `ffprobe` (an argument array, a 60 second timeout, two at a time: duration, codecs, audio and subtitle streams, embedded tags, FairPlay detection) and matches it on TMDB with the owner's Read Access Token (`TMDB_READ_TOKEN`) by title, year and a runtime within eight minutes of the file's. Confident matches get their overview, genres, poster, backdrop, directors, top ten cast and collection; the rest keep their top five candidates and are listed as needing a match. A rescan adds new files, follows renames, marks vanished files missing without losing watch history, and retries films TMDB could not answer; two files for one film become two editions. Embedded `mov_text` subtitles are served as WebVTT (`GET /api/media-files/{id}/subtitles/{streamIndex}.vtt`, extracted with `ffmpeg` and cached under `CINEFILM_CACHE_DIR`), and the player adds `<track>` elements and turns the forced English track on by itself when Subtitles is Off. Scans run at start-up on an empty library, nightly at 04:30 London time, and from Admin, whose placeholder is replaced by the Library scan panel (live progress, counts, Scan now, 409 when a scan runs) and Films needing attention (needs a match with its candidates, protected, needs conversion, several files for one film), with the TMDB credit. The empty library's buttons now lead to Scan now. `TMDB_API_KEY` is renamed `TMDB_READ_TOKEN` throughout, and CI installs `ffmpeg` for the real-tool test.
