### Changed — credits

The credits analysis now reads the credits text real films have. A mostly dark frame measures its text against its own brightest pixels (never below half or above the whole window's peak), so thin or dim lettering counts; frames with 70% dark pixels (dense multi-column credits) can be text; a frame needs only 30% of its bright pixels to be text lines; and text sitting almost entirely in the bottom band, such as burned-in subtitles, is not credits. Moving credits are recognised more readily: the row profiles are smoothed over three rows so small, slowly moving text still matches, a third of the frames showing text is enough, a crawl may match its shifted row profile with an error of up to 0.25 (it was 0.15), and card changes as small as a thousandth of the picture count (a pair is still only at or under 0.0005). A card change must also replace more than half of the larger frame's bright pixels, which rejects small changes against a lit scene (a window switching on, a figure stepping); a run of consecutive changes counts as one card change. Lit scenes that look like text can still be misread, and the phase 2b library check measures that. Nothing is applied yet: the analysis still only reports, and the reader and re-analysis of stored results follow in phase 2b's next part.
